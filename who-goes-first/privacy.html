GT-Apps Who Goes First? Privacy Support

Last updated: 31 October 2026

The short version. Your players’ names and photos stay on your phone — they are never uploaded anywhere. There are no accounts. The free version shows a small banner ad supplied by Google AdMob, which collects some device data to show and measure ads (you can say no to personalised ads). If you buy “Remove ads”, the purchase is handled by Apple or Google, and RevenueCat confirms it to the app. Paying users see no ads, and the ad service is never started for them.

Who we are

Who Goes First? (“the app”) is published by GT-Apps, the trading name of an independent developer in the United Kingdom (“we”, “us”), who is the data controller for this policy. For anything about this policy or your data, email support@gt-apps.co.uk.

Information that stays on your device

The app stores the following only on your phone, in its own private storage:

  • Player names, their wheel colours, and who is switched on for tonight.
  • Player photos you choose to add (copied into the app and cropped to a circle).
  • A note of whether ads have been removed, so the app knows straight away.

We cannot see any of this. It is not sent to us or to anyone else, and it is deleted when you delete the app. Device backups (iCloud or Google) may include it, under your own backup settings.

Camera and photo library

The app asks for access only when you choose to take or pick a photo for a player. The chosen photo is saved inside the app on your device. The app does not read, upload or scan any other photos.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The free version shows a single banner ad on the player line-up and player list screens. Ads are provided by Google AdMob. To show ads, limit how often you see the same one, prevent fraud and measure performance, Google may collect:

  • Device identifiers — on Android the advertising ID; on iPhone an app-specific identifier (we do not ask for Apple’s tracking permission, so the advertising identifier used for cross-app tracking is not shared).
  • IP address and approximate location derived from it.
  • Device and app information, and how you interact with ads.
  • Crash and performance data from the ad software.

Ads in the app are limited to content suitable for general audiences.

Your consent choices

If you are in the UK, the European Economic Area or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before ads are personalised, using Google’s consent tool. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy choices in the app. If you decline, you may still see non-personalised ads, which use limited data for purposes such as fraud prevention and frequency capping.

Learn more about how Google uses data: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Purchases (“Remove ads”)

“Remove ads” is a one-off in-app purchase. Payment is handled entirely by the Apple App Store or Google Play — we never see your card or payment details. To confirm the purchase and support “Restore purchases”, the app uses RevenueCat, which receives the store’s purchase receipt together with a random, anonymous app user ID (not your name or email). If you remove ads, the advertising software is not started at all.

What we do not do

  • No accounts, sign-ups or email collection.
  • No analytics or tracking added by us.
  • We do not sell your data, and we have no access to player names or photos.

Legal basis (UK GDPR)

Personalised advertising relies on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in the app. Non-personalised ads and fraud prevention rely on our legitimate interest in funding a free app. Purchase verification is necessary to provide what you bought (contract).

Children

The app is a family games-night tool meant to be operated by adults or teenagers. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. Ads are restricted to general-audience content.

Your rights

Data on your device is under your control: edit or delete players in the app, or delete the app to remove everything. For data held by Google or RevenueCat, you can use the controls they provide or contact us and we will help. UK users may also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk).

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the date at the top.
